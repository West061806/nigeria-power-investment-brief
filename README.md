# Nigeria Power Investment Brief

## Overview
An analysis of the Nigeria's power sector, specifically the generation capacity gaps, distribution performance, billing efficiency, and renewable energy potential across the 2021–2024 period, to identify priority investments.

## Key Findings
1. Nigeria's connected power plants ran at 49.3% of its installed capacity in 2024, with 4,650MW effectively unavailable due to poor plant availability.
2. Out of the eleven discos, six of them received less power in 2024 than 2021, with Kaduna and Yola each declining by 28.5%.
3. The solar irradiance of 6.22kWh/(m^2) recorded in the Northern states was higher than the 4.78kWh/(m^2) recorded in the South, yet the electricity access in the North trails heavily when compared to the South at 31% compared to 61.8% recorded in the South.

## Investment Recommendations
1. Immediate rehabilitation for the underperforming power plants, particularly Egbin (1,320 MW installed, 42% availability) and Delta (900 MW, 39% availability).
2. Improvement in the billing infrastructures of the Kaduna and Abuja discos, as the discos with the highest energy losses
3. Large scale investment in the solar energy infrastructures in areas with low energy access and higher solar irradiance, such as Sokoto, Borno and Kano.

## Data Source
Nigerian Electricity Regulatory Commission (NERC) Annual Report 2024, Central Bank of Nigeria (CBN), National Bureau of Statistics (NBS), World Bank
